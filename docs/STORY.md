# The Letter from Paris

## 1. The flight over

Secret code: **BONJOUR**

Viv is a private detective. People come to her with puzzles that nobody else can solve.

One morning a letter comes. The stamp is from France, a country far away across the sea.

> Chère Viv,  
> Someone wants to steal a painting from my museum. I need the best detective in the world. Please come to Paris! Your plane leaves today.  
> Monsieur Noir  
> Director, Musée d'Orsay

*"Chère" means Dear. A museum is a building that keeps famous paintings safe so everyone can see them.*

Paris is the biggest city in France. The people there speak French, and Viv does not know a single word of it.

She opens her notebook. There is just enough time to learn a few words before the plane leaves.

### Notebook: Hello and names

- **Hello and goodbye.** Bonjour (Hello); Au revoir (Goodbye); Merci (Thank you); S'il vous plaît (Please); Pardon (Sorry / Excuse me); Oui (Yes); Non (No)
- **Meeting people.** Madame (Ma'am); Monsieur (Sir); Je m'appelle… (My name is…); Comment vous appelez-vous ? (What is your name?); Parlez-vous anglais ? (Do you speak English?); Je ne comprends pas. (I don't understand.)
- **Asking for help.** Old words come back.

- *Tip: Bonjour comes first.* In France you say Bonjour before you ask for anything: in a shop, at a desk, on a bus. If you forget, people may think you are rude.
- *Tip: Madame and Monsieur.* Call a grown-up woman Madame and a grown-up man Monsieur. "Bonjour, Madame" is the polite way to say hello.
- *Tip: Try French first.* It is polite to try French first, even if you get it wrong. After that you can ask: Parlez-vous anglais ?

*Hello, goodbye and your name are in your notebook. Next: the airline desk.*

### Notebook: Numbers to 20

- **One to five.** un (1 (one)); deux (2 (two)); trois (3 (three)); quatre (4 (four)); cinq (5 (five))
- **Six to ten.** six (6 (six)); sept (7 (seven)); huit (8 (eight)); neuf (9 (nine)); dix (10 (ten))
- **Eleven to twenty.** onze (11 (eleven)); douze (12 (twelve)); treize (13 (thirteen)); quatorze (14 (fourteen)); quinze (15 (fifteen)); seize (16 (sixteen)); dix-sept (17 (seventeen)); dix-huit (18 (eighteen)); dix-neuf (19 (nineteen)); vingt (20 (twenty))
- **To the hotel.** le taxi (the taxi); l'hôtel (the hotel); Je vais à… (I am going to…); la rue (the street)

- *Tip: The snail of Paris.* Paris is cut into 20 numbered parts called arrondissements. Number 1 is in the middle. The rest wind outward like a snail's shell.
- *Tip: Ten and a bit.* Dix-sept is ten-seven: 17. Dix-huit is ten-eight: 18. Dix-neuf is ten-nine: 19. The numbers from 11 to 16 each have their own word.
- *Tip: A French address.* Rue means street and place means square. The house number comes first: 15 place Vendôme.
- *Tip: The light on a taxi.* A green light on a taxi roof means it is free. A red light means someone is already in it.

### Notebook: Days and times

- **The days.** lundi (Monday); mardi (Tuesday); mercredi (Wednesday); jeudi (Thursday); vendredi (Friday); samedi (Saturday); dimanche (Sunday); aujourd'hui (today); demain (tomorrow)

- **What time is it?.** Quelle heure est-il ? (What time is it?); vingt heures (eight o'clock at night)

- *Tip: Days and dates.* French days do not start with a capital letter. Dates put the day before the month: 14/7 is the 14th of July.
- *Tip: After twenty.* After vingt you just add the small number: vingt-deux, vingt-trois, vingt-quatre. Only 21 and 31 slip in an extra word: vingt et un, trente et un.

### Notebook: Tonight

- **Tonight.** ce soir (tonight, this evening); On va… (We are going… / Let's go…); aller au spectacle (to go to a show); aller danser (to go dancing); aller au cinéma (to go to the cinema); aller au restaurant (to go to a restaurant)
